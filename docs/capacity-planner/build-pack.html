3How the forecast works

Capacity is each person's weekly hours less holidays. Demand is live projects plus open deals, with each deal weighted by its chance of winning and spread evenly over its delivery dates.

Capacity

30 hours a week per person by default, set per person on the roster. Approved holidays and regional public holidays come off. Pending holidays show as tentative.

Demand

Live projects count their remaining hours. Open deals count hours × win probability. Customer success hours come from CS projects.

The gap

Demand minus capacity, per person, team, region and type of work, shown in hours, % full and FTE.

Worked example

A 60-hour job starting Tuesday 1 December, six weeks long, at 20% to win, counts as 60 × 0.2 = 12 hours. That is 0.4 hours for each of its 30 working days: 1.6 h in the week of 30 November, 2.0 h in each of the next five weeks and 0.4 h on Monday 11 January. When it is won, it counts in full.

Different kinds of work spread differently

  • Fixed projects: evenly over the delivery window, five working days a week.
  • Flexible drawdown: evenly over the term. If there is no end date, it is assumed to be six months.
  • Managed services: the agreed hours each month, as a steady load.

Four ways to view it

Leaders plan on the expected value but hire against the risk. So every screen can switch between four views: Committed (won work only), Weighted (the default), Best case and Full pipeline. If a deal has no probability, a default for its stage is used. It is marked "estimated", and the rep is asked to fill it in.

Who carries the work

Each project's hours go to the people assigned to it. Hour-based assignments are taken first, then percentage splits. With no assignments, the project owner carries the work. Anything unassigned still counts in team totals, with the skills, level and region it needs. From Phase 3, the planner ranks the best fit for each job and suggests moves when someone is overbooked.

6Assistants and meeting follow-up

Claude assistants tell each person, in Slack, what needs their attention and what to do about it. Every message is one click from the fix.

What people receive
  • Morning briefs for ICs and team leads.
  • Nudges for missing information, at the moment it matters.
  • Early warnings of future clashes and overbooking.
  • A /capacity question box in Slack.
  • Monthly gap insights for leadership.
How we keep it useful and safe
  • Fixed rules decide the facts. Claude only writes the words, so it never invents figures.
  • At most 3 alerts and 1 digest per person a day, with quiet hours by region.
  • Everyone can mute, snooze or change their digest time.
  • Messages come from one "boost Delivery Assistant" bot, not from a person.

Meeting follow-up

After each meeting recorded by the monday AI Notetaker, internal attendees get a short Slack summary. Each summary has the decisions, the risks and the actions with owners. One click creates the actions. Suggested updates to the deal or project are shown for review in the app and saved under the reviewer's name. Nothing changes without a person's say-so.

This reuses the transcript integration boost already runs in Sally Gecko, on the same contractual terms. The two never update the same fields, so a sales call never gets two conflicting follow-ups.

Running cost

Claude Sonnet writes the everyday messages. Claude Opus handles meeting analysis and open questions, where accuracy matters most. The total is about $165 a month at forecast volume, tracked per assistant.

8Risks, decisions and next steps

The biggest risk is thin data at launch, not technology. The plan tackles it with a data clean-up fortnight, honest "estimated" markers and role-specific missing-data lists.

Key decisions in this proposal

  • Built into the SDR OS app, not monday's native Workload view, which can't weight pipeline or read holiday data.
  • A new Capacity Roster, so the private HR board is never read.
  • Weighted forecasting with four views, and stage defaults where a probability is missing.
  • Everyone can see every screen. Editing is limited by role and protected by monday's own permissions.
  • Changes suggested from meetings need a person's review and are saved in that person's name.

B1.6 Who carries the demand

  1. Allocations first (Phase 2). Each allocation row gives a person part of the work item's demand. It can be limited to its own date range, for example BA on site in weeks 1 and 2 only. Hours-per-week allocations are taken out of the item's daily demand first, capped at that demand, so they never add hours the item does not have. One that exceeds the item's demand is flagged "Over-allocated". Share % allocations then take their share of what is left. Shares on one item may not total more than 100%. The app blocks that, and a manual edit on the board is flagged.
  2. The remainder goes to owners. Any daily demand not covered by share allocations is split evenly across the item's default carriers:
    • for projects, the Project Owner people who are on the roster;
    • for deals, the proposed IC/CSM people, marked tentative.
  3. Off-roster owners. If an owner is not on the roster (for example a salesperson), their part is reported as "Off-roster". It is not counted against anyone's capacity, and it is flagged for data quality.
  4. No carrier. The demand is unassigned. It counts in team totals and gaps, and it lists the required skills, level and region so the right person can be found.

Phase 1: coverage ratio. For each category and week:

  • Demand is the category's demand for that week.
  • Qualified capacity is the total capacity of the people who meet the category's level and skill rule.
  • Coverage is demand divided by qualified capacity. Above 85% is flagged amber, and above 100% red.

This is easy to explain, but it overstates supply when one person qualifies for several categories.

Phase 3: exact shortfall (max-flow). Each week we solve a small flow network: source → category (demand) → qualified person (∞) → sink (capacity). The maximum flow is the most demand the team could cover if work were reassigned perfectly. A category's shortfall is its demand minus the flow through it. That shortfall cannot be fixed by rebalancing. It needs hiring, training or moving dates, which is exactly the CEO's "train people on MS" signal. With 15 categories × 60 people the graph solves in under 5 ms in the browser.

B1.10 Move suggestions (Phase 3)

When it runs. Person X is over capacity in weeks W, or X is needed for a new job J and is short by H hours.

  1. List movable work. Take X's allocations and default-owned items that overlap W.
    • Skip anything marked Locked.
    • Skip on-site work X is the only one qualified to do.
    • Skip the Face role on projects that start or end within move.face_lock_weeks (2). Continuity matters most there.
  2. Find a substitute. For each candidate piece of work, run the ranking for its own requirements, with X excluded. Keep the best substitute Y whose free hours in W cover at least 80% of that work.
  3. Score the disruption. Each move gets a disruption cost by role (move.cost.*): Shadow 0.5, Offline 1, BA 2, Face 3. Add 1 if X logged a client call on that project in the last 7 days (Sprint "Activity Type (IC's)" is Kickoff, Discovery, Sync or Consultation Call).
  4. Rank and choose. Rank moves by hours freed ÷ disruption cost. Pick moves greedily until H is covered, or show the best partial plan.
  5. Apply as tentative. "Apply" creates tentative allocations for Y and ends X's. The team lead confirms them, and nothing changes silently.

DS-009 · Capacity Snapshots P4

Entity: one person × one snapshot week × one horizon bucket. Buckets are weeks 1 to 4, 5 to 8 and 9 to 12. That gives ~180 rows a week, ~9.4k a year, well inside the 100k board limit (S-10). A sibling board, Deal Probability Snapshots (DS-014), holds one row per open services deal per week (deal, stage, probability, hours), ~120 a week, for calibration.

WF-09 · Data quality loop

Rules DQ-01 to DQ-24 feed three places: each person's My Missing Data, the scorecard for sales leaders and team leads, and the Overview "Estimated share" figure, which is the % of demand hours relying on a fallback. Target: under 15% by the end of Phase 1 and under 5% by the end of Phase 2. The Tuesday and Monday rituals in WF-03 close the loop.

WF-10 · Settings governance

Only Admins edit Capacity Settings. Every change is visible in the board activity log. Leadership agrees changes to stage probabilities or bands at the monthly review. Phase 4 calibration (FR-162) proposes new stage defaults each quarter.

WF-11 · Public holidays: yearly load

Each December an admin runs P-08's import for the coming year. UK dates come from gov.uk's bank-holidays JSON (England & Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland). Israel, US and UAE dates are loaded from a verified public holiday API, or entered by hand from the government calendar where the API's coverage is not confirmed (A-09). Company days are added by hand.

C7.5 The back-end in practice

  • Hosting. The same monday app gains server-side code (Node) deployed with mapps code:push, in the region that matches the account's data region (Q-09). The scheduler is not available in monday's IL hosting region (S-16).

  • Endpoints.
    • POST /mndy-cronjob/tick: the hourly scheduler entry point.
    • POST /api/refresh: manual refresh. It checks the session token (signed with the client secret), the role from the roster and the cool-down.
    • POST /api/delta: after an app save. It takes {board, itemId} and re-reads that item.
    • GET /api/status: build progress.

D2Build steps

In brief

The ordered instructions the build follows, phase by phase. These are written for the build team rather than for review.

These are the prompts to give Claude, in order, to build the whole thing. Each one is self-contained. It says what to read, what to change, which constraints apply, how to prove it works and what to report back. Why the prompts are sequential

The build crosses three kinds of gate that a single long run cannot pass on its own:

  • Your approval of monday writes. Each board or column creation through the monday connector shows a preview first.
  • Human data work. Skills ratings, timelines and deal fields have to be filled in by people.
  • UAT sign-off. Each persona has to accept the phase.

Within a phase, Claude can run several prompts back to back. Between phases, a person has to say "go".
